## Intro

The Arc Robotics / RoboMaster competition is an intense robotics competition where custom-built robots face off in tactical combat. A key aspect of the competition is the "armor plates" equipped on each robot, which register hits from projectiles.

## Actual Project Work

### Dataset Collection

There were no previous datasets collected for armor plates specifically for the Arc Robotics competition, so we had to collect our own. We split the dataset into two: one for identifying plates in an image, and one for classifying the plate's icon.

Our plate detection dataset was created by taking photos of our robots with plates attached in different scenarios. We ran into an initial problem of overfitting to black blobs since all our robots were black. We added a supplementary dataset of just floating plates which eliminated the bias.

The icon dataset was generated by using a trained plate detection model, and then grayscaling everything to make it more uniform.

### Labeling and Autolabeling

We used SAM3 with hand-crafted prompts to help auto-label the data, drastically speeding up our workflow.

### Model Selection and Training

Our hardware is a Jetson Orin AGX, with a pair of Basler cameras. Since these cameras can go up to 160 fps+, we wanted our whole pipeline to be able to reach at least 120 fps. Because of this, we had a strong priority on inference speed.

- **Plate Detection**: We went with a yolo26n model, seeing that TensorRT optimizations on a T4 chip brought inference down to around 3ms on their website.
- **Icon Classification**: We ended up going with a small custom CNN with 7 layers. We evaluated the yolo26n-cls model as well but found that it was much larger than what we needed for simple classifying.

We trained the models on Google Colab, using Comet as a visualizer to check training and finetuning parameters as needed.

### Implementation

After training the models, we exported them as `.engine` files through TensorRT and tested their real-world performance.
